For students: turn your own class material into a study plan

How it works

01

Set up your class

Name the class, set the number of weeks, and choose a grade level from Elementary School to Professional Education. Then enter the first day of class and the dates of your tests.

02

Add your material

Drop in a PDF, a Word file, a slide deck, or a text file, or add an audio or video recording of the lecture itself. Tell Study Guide what the teacher stressed, and list what did not make sense so the plan can slow down at that point in the study schedule. Each file is processed automatically after it uploads.

03

Create your study plan

Pick the class and the week, and a lesson, an interactive podcast, and a schedule of study sessions are built for you. Every plan stays available, so you can return to a lesson again before the next test.

What one chapter becomes

A lesson is written from your own file first, and the key terms are defined where they appear. The interactive podcast reads that lesson aloud, asks a question, and then gives the answer, which lets you check your recall on a commute or on a short walk.

Interactive Chapter Podcast

Listen to a lesson overview designed for recall and audio reinforcement.

0:00 / 5:48

Sessions that deepen

Study sessions are arranged at three levels: normal, deep thinking, and deeper thinking. Each level has a follow-up session for the topics you missed, and the whole schedule can also be viewed as a list, a calendar, or a combined calendar across your classes.

1. Normal Session • Core recall and basic application
2. Deep Thinking • Edge cases, multi-step problems, and analysis
3. Deeper Thinking • Synthesis across chapters and novel problem transfer

Your teacher's emphasis, on the record

The plan is shaped by two optional questions, whether the teacher stressed anything and whether anything failed to make sense, and Study Guide slows down on the second, so the plan reflects your lecture and your gaps instead of a standard outline for the whole class.

Teacher Emphasis 75%
Learning Gap / Confusion 40%
Calculated Study Time: +3 Targeted Revision Sessions

Built from what you bring

Study Guide works from the material you add, so a single product can serve a school subject, an entrance exam, or a professional qualification, depending on the files you add. The plan is anchored to your syllabus and your sources because they are the only inputs that the product uses.

Step 01

Define your course

Name the course, set its length and grade level, and add the notes, slides, files, or lecture recordings that you already teach from.

Step 02

Assign it to your students

Choose the students who should take the course, and it is delivered to each of them, so the whole group begins from one shared course.

Step 03

Follow each student's progress

Open each student's progress whenever you need it, and see how every student is doing without waiting for the next test to tell you.

One course, many paces

Each student works through the course at a pace that suits them, so a quick learner is not held back and a slower one is not hurried past a topic that needs time. Questions adapt to the answers a student gives, which keeps practice close to what that student still has to learn.

Progress you can read

Progress is shown student by student, and the next session with a student can begin from the topic that needs attention instead of a general review.

Your material, your course

The course is built from the files and recordings you teach from, so it follows your syllabus and carries your emphasis, and nothing has to be rewritten into a new format before your students can use it.

For one tutor or a whole center

An independent tutor with a handful of students and a coaching class with several batches can use the same tools, since a course is defined once and assigned whenever it is needed.
